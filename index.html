Summary

We will build an end-to-end pipeline that will turn a handful of casually captured, unposed 2D photos into a real-time 3D Gaussian splat of a scene. The original 3D Gaussian Splatting method [1] has four stages: structure-from-motion, conversion to Gaussians, training, and differentiable rasterization. We will keep this framing but replace the calibrated multi-view capture requirement with a pose-free pointmap front-end. The Gaussian representation will then be initialized and trained with our own optimizer, densification, and pruning logic. The final deliverable will be a WebGL viewer demo plus quantitative novel-view-synthesis results measured on scenes we will capture ourselves.

Problem Description

3D Gaussian Splatting [1] renders photorealistic scenes in real time, but the standard pipeline assumes dense, posed multi-view input, typically 50–300 photos together with camera intrinsics and extrinsics from COLMAP's structure-from-motion. Few casual users can meet that requirement. Most people have a handful of phone snapshots rather than a calibrated capture rig.

The challenge is twofold. First, structure-from-motion degrades badly on sparse, unposed input because few feature matches yield unreliable poses, and without poses there is nothing to optimize against. Second, 3DGS training itself degenerates when only a few views are available, since the densification schedule and photometric loss overfit the training views and hallucinate geometry in unobserved regions.

Our general approach will follow recent pose-free sparse-view work such as InstantSplat [5], NoPoSplat [8], and MV-DUSt3R+ [9]. We will use a pretrained pointmap model, DUSt3R [3] or MASt3R [4], as the SfM-equivalent front-end, in the same role COLMAP played in the original 3DGS paper. This model regresses dense pixel-aligned 3D pointmaps and camera parameters directly from unposed images. We will then convert the pointmap into per-pixel Gaussian initializations and train the representation with our own 3DGS-style optimizer on top of the differentiable rasterizer.

Goals and Deliverables

Questions we plan to answer

  • How few casually captured, unposed photos do we need before the splat becomes unusable? We will sweep 2, 4, and 8 input views on the same scene.
  • Does a dense pointmap initialization (DUSt3R [3]/MASt3R [4]) beat sparse SfM (COLMAP) initialization when both feed the same 3DGS optimizer?
  • Where does the pose-free pipeline fail on scenes like textureless walls, reflective surfaces, or large environments? Can depth regularization rescue the worst cases?

Must deliver

  • An end-to-end pipeline that will take 2–8 unposed photos of a scene and produce a real-time 3D Gaussian splat, which we will demonstrate on 3–5 test scenes we will capture ourselves.
  • Our graphics contribution will be the Gaussian initialization from pointmaps, the training loop over position, covariance, color, and opacity, and the densification and pruning schedule. The differentiable CUDA rasterizer will come from gsplat, and we will state this division of labor explicitly rather than burying it.
  • We will report quantitative results, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on held-out views of each scene, and will present them as plots across the view-count sweep.
  • We will build a WebGL viewer so the final splats can be explored live in the browser during our presentation demo, and will include still renders of every scene on this page.

Hope to deliver

  • We hope to support single-image input by generating pseudo-views with a diffusion model before splatting.
  • We hope to deploy a web demo where TAs and classmates can upload their own photos and get back a splat.
  • We hope to add a compression/compactness analysis of the trained splat (Gaussian count vs. quality trade-off).

Software and starting point

  • We will use gsplat only as the differentiable CUDA rasterization engine. Our optimizer, densification logic, and metrics will be written by us.
  • We will use pretrained DUSt3R/MASt3R checkpoints for the front-end, playing the same role COLMAP plays in the original 3DGS pipeline.
  • We will adapt a WebGL splat viewer (e.g. antimatter15/splat style) for the in-browser demo.
  • We will start from these libraries rather than from existing project code. Everything between the pointmaps and the rendered splats will be written by us.

Computing platform

  • Team GPU access is not yet confirmed. Our plan is to use a local CUDA GPU if available and to fall back to Colab or a rented cloud GPU (AutoDL) otherwise. We will keep scenes small so training fits within a single modest GPU's budget.
